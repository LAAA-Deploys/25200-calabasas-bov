Executive Summary

Commercially Zoned Land in Calabasas
2.54 acres with road frontage, next to an operating care facility, a short drive from the Las Virgenes interchange
Most vacant land around Calabasas is zoned for one estate home. This parcel is zoned CL (Commercial, Limited), which opens it to office, medical, day care, nursery and institutional users as well as the neighboring facility and conservation buyers.

The property is a 110,506 SF (2.54 acre) vacant parcel on the south side of Calabasas Road, the frontage road along the 101 Freeway, at the west end of the street. It has about 219 feet of frontage on Calabasas Road and runs about 450 feet south into the hillside. The front of the lot near the road is the most level ground; the rear rises roughly 50 to 60 feet. It borders the skilled nursing facility at 25100 Calabasas Road to the east and open hillside to the west and south.

Comparable hillside land without approved plans has traded from $0.88 to $3.83 per SF over the past three years; buildable estate pads with utilities sold for $7 to $8 per SF. The parcel's commercial zoning and paved road frontage support pricing at the top of the hillside range. We recommend a list price of $375,000 ($3.39 per SF, about $147,800 per acre), with an expected sale range of $275,000 to $350,000.

Zoning and Site Considerations

CL, Commercial, Limited. Calabasas applies the CL zone to commercial land with access limits or sensitive environmental features that cannot support the full range of commercial uses. Its intended uses include limited retail and commercial services, restaurants, plant nurseries, and business and professional offices. It matches the General Plan's Business, Limited Intensity designation, which caps floor area at 0.20 FAR, or about 22,100 SF of building on this lot before setbacks, slope, oak and fire requirements. The 2025 MLS listing also cited day care, medical office, studio, church and nonprofit office uses, per the City's Planning Department.

What a buyer will study. Calabasas regulates oak trees closely, and much of the western half is under oak canopy, so the buildable area will likely center on the more open ground near the road and along the eastern edge. The prior listing noted that an engineered driveway is needed to reach the upper building area. The parcel is in a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone, and the Assessor shows no sewer connection. A Scenic Corridor overlay applies to the adjoining parcel along Calabasas Road and likely applies here as well, which adds design review.

To confirm before marketing: the zoning and any overlay with the Calabasas Planning Division (our zoning reading comes from SCAG's 2024 regional parcel data and the Calabasas code summary); sewer or septic feasibility and water service from Las Virgenes Municipal Water District; a tree survey; and whether the adjoining open land to the west is held by a conservation agency, as the prior listing stated.

Key Takeaways

1
Hillside land without plans trades at about $1 to $4 per SF. The seven constrained sales averaged $2.27 per SF, and the closest match in size and timing, 25066 Mulholland, closed in October 2026 at $3.06 per SF.
2
Commercial zoning sets this parcel apart. Nearly every comparable is zoned for one estate home. CL zoning lets office, medical, day care and nursery users bid, which supports the top of the hillside range.
3
The 2025 market test points to about $400,000. At a $399,000 asking price the parcel went contingent after about four and a half months. Earlier asks of $650,000 and $750,000 did not sell.
4
Buildable pads sell for twice as much. Flat, usable lots sold at $7 to $8 per SF. A buyer who confirms a building area, a driveway route and utility service closes some of that gap, which is the upside a marketing campaign sells.
5
The neighbor is the natural buyer. The care facility next door has the most to gain, and we would approach it directly before and during marketing.

Why $375,000. The seven hillside sales without plans averaged $2.27 per SF, or about $251,000 applied to this lot. The subject should trade above that average for three reasons: commercial zoning, which none of the estate lots have; paved frontage on Calabasas Road next to an operating facility; and a neighbor with a clear reason to buy. At $3.39 per SF the asking price sits just above 25066 Mulholland ($3.06) and just below the 2025 asking price that drew a contingent buyer ($3.61).

What moves the number. Confirming a building area, a driveway route, and water and sewer or septic service before marketing would let us present the parcel closer to a buildable pad and test a higher price. If the City limits uses more than the zoning suggests, or the tree survey shrinks the usable area, the value moves toward the hillside average and the conservation buyers.

The recommendation assumes the parcel is sold as is, with no approvals, and that title conveys clean after the 2025 foreclosure. Measure ULA does not apply; the parcel is in the City of Calabasas, not the City of Los Angeles.
